When Is a Horse in Need?

In addition to the situations below, the Icelandic horses’ high market value can create a greater financial incentive for resale, even when a horse’s underlying needs have not been fully understood or addressed. A calm or stoic outward appearance can make pain, discomfort, and emotional distress less obvious, allowing a horse’s struggles to be overlooked or minimized.

As a result, buyers may unknowingly take on a horse requiring substantial veterinary care, rehabilitation, and financial support. A valuable horse is not necessarily a healthy horse, and a quiet horse is not necessarily a comfortable one. We also consider these types of situations a horse in need.

  1. Neglect

    Inadequate food, water, shelter, hoof care, dental care, or necessary veterinary treatment.

  2. Abuse or mistreatment

    A horse experiencing physical harm or handling that threatens its welfare.

  3. Abandonment

    A horse left behind or without anyone taking responsibility for its care.

  4. Owner financial hardship

    The owner can no longer afford essential care and needs help or a safe surrender option.

  5. Owner illness, disability, aging, or death

    The person responsible can no longer care for the horse, and no suitable caregiver is available.

  6. Loss of a home or boarding situation

    Moving, loss of property, or a barn closure leaves the horse without a safe place to go.

  7. Medical needs beyond the owner’s resources

    An injury, chronic condition, or disability requires care the owner cannot provide.

  8. Senior, injured and nonrideable horses without a secure future

    A horse needs retirement or a companion home, but appropriate care is unavailable.

  9. Behavioral or training challenges that jeopardize placement

    A horse needing patient handling and training, or rehabilitation to live safely in a suitable home with a well matched owner.

  10. Credible risk of entering the slaughter pipeline or in a kill pen

    Intervention is needed to secure a safe placement either temporary or permanent.

  11. Displacement during an emergency

    Fire, flooding, or another disaster creates a need for temporary shelter or longer-term placement.
